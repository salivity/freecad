Using the FreeCAD Part Design Groove Tool

The Groove tool in FreeCAD's Part Design workbench is a subtractive modeling feature used to carve out material from an existing solid body by revolving a 2D sketch around a chosen axis. This article explains the core purpose of the Groove tool, how it functions in mechanical design workflows, typical use cases, and the essential steps required to apply it correctly in your projects.

Core Function of the Groove Tool

In FreeCAD Part Design, the Groove tool acts as the exact subtractive counterpart to the Revolution tool. While a Revolution adds material by revolving a profile around a centerline, a Groove removes material along a rotational path. It operates conceptually like a lathe cutting tool, clearing a swept volume out of an already established 3D feature.

Common Engineering Use Cases

The Groove tool is primarily utilized on cylindrical or rotational geometry where features must sweep along a continuous circumference. Typical applications include:

  • O-Ring and Seal Seats: Cutting precise, rectangular, or rounded channels inside or outside cylindrical bodies to house elastomer seals.
  • Retaining Ring Slots: Machining narrow recesses for circlips or snap rings on shafts and inside bores.
  • Pulleys and Sheaves: Carving V-belt, round-belt, or cable profiles into cylindrical blanks.
  • Shaft Undercuts and Reliefs: Modeling stress-relief grooves, thread relief necks, and bearing seats on turned shafts.

How to Use the Groove Tool

To execute a groove successfully, the model must meet specific conditions:

  1. Active Body with Existing Solid: Because the Groove tool is subtractive, there must be pre-existing solid geometry within the active Body to cut into.
  2. Sketch Creation: Create a closed sketch on a plane intersecting the rotational axis (such as an axis-aligned datum plane or a standard coordinate plane). The sketch represents the cross-section of the material you want to remove.
  3. Axis Alignment: The sketch geometry must stay on one side of the intended axis of revolution; crossing the axis creates self-intersecting geometry and causes the feature to fail.
  4. Applying the Feature: Select the sketch and click the Groove icon. In the task panel, define the Axis of revolution (e.g., Sketch axis, standard X/Y/Z axis, or a custom edge/datum line) and specify the Angle (defaulting to 360° for a full revolution, or smaller angles for partial cuts).

Key Rules and Best Practices

  • Keep Sketches Closed: The sketch must be a fully closed wire. Open profiles cannot generate a valid subtractive solid.
  • Avoid Zero-Thickness Faces: Ensure the profile cleanly intersects or clears the intended boundaries to prevent non-manifold geometry errors.
  • Use Construction Lines: Utilizing construction geometry within your sketch helps lock references to the model's primary centerlines without interfering with the profile's boundary.

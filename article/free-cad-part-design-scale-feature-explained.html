FreeCAD Part Design Scale Feature Explained

The Scale feature in FreeCAD’s Part Design workbench allows users to resize an entire solid body or a specific feature proportionally or along independent axes. This article covers what the tool does, how it functions within the Part Design workflow, its primary settings, practical use cases such as manufacturing compensation, and critical considerations when using it in parametric modeling.

Understanding the Scale Feature

In FreeCAD, the Scale tool modifies the geometric dimensions of a solid model based on a specified multiplier. Instead of requiring you to edit every individual sketch constraint and extrusion depth manually, the Scale feature recalculates the outer geometry of a solid relative to a specific reference point.

Key Capabilities and Settings

The Scale tool in Part Design provides several configurations depending on design requirements:

  • Uniform Scaling: Scales the geometry equally along the X, Y, and Z axes using a single scaling factor. A factor greater than 1 increases the part size, while a factor between 0 and 1 reduces it.
  • Non-Uniform (Non-Isotropic) Scaling: Allows independent scaling factors for each individual axis (X, Y, and Z). This is useful for stretching or flattening parts along a specific direction without altering the others.
  • Center of Scale: Defines the anchor point from which the expansion or contraction occurs. Typically, this can be the global origin (0, 0, 0), the center of mass of the feature, or a custom vertex/datum point.

Practical Use Cases

1. Material Shrinkage and Expansion Compensation

Certain manufacturing processes, such as plastic injection molding, metal casting, and 3D printing, cause materials to shrink as they cool. Designers use the Scale feature to increase the size of the final model slightly (e.g., applying a 1.02 scale for 2% shrinkage) so the finished manufactured part matches the intended final dimensions.

2. Rapid Prototyping and Model Scale Conversions

When building physical display models, scale models (such as 1:24 or 1:100 scale), or scaled test prototypes, the Scale tool allows for instant conversions without redesigning the underlying sketches.

3. Modifying Imported CAD Geometry

Non-parametric imported files, such as STEP or IGES models, lack an editable sketch history. The Scale feature provides a direct way to resize these static solids within the Part Design container.

Limitations and Best Practices

  • Loss of Geometric Intent with Non-Uniform Scaling: Applying different scale factors along axes will distort circular features into ellipses, which can make cylindrical bores, screw threads, or standard pin holes unusable for hardware assembly.
  • Parametric Considerations: The Scale tool creates a new step in the model tree. Downstream features applied to scaled bodies will build upon the scaled dimensions, while upstream sketches remain at their original defined constraints. Where strict parametric integrity is required, modifying the underlying sketch dimensions or using spreadsheet-driven variables is often preferable to post-process scaling.

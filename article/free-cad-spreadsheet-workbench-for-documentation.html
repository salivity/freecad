FreeCAD Spreadsheet Workbench for Documentation

The FreeCAD Spreadsheet workbench serves as a critical bridge between 3D parametric geometry and technical documentation, enabling designers to organize, calculate, and present project data efficiently. This article explores how the Spreadsheet workbench functions as a documentation tool, detailing its role in generating dynamic bills of materials, integrating with technical drawing sheets, logging engineering parameters, and exporting structured data for external reporting.

Dynamic Bills of Materials (BOM)

A primary documentation purpose of the Spreadsheet workbench is compiling dynamic Bills of Materials and parts lists. Designers can aggregate metadata from 3D model components—such as part names, quantities, dimensions, material types, and weights—directly into a structured table. Because the spreadsheet integrates with the parametric engine, any changes made to the 3D model (such as resizing an object or adding components) automatically update in the spreadsheet, preventing discrepancies between the design and its documentation.

Seamless Integration with TechDraw

For formal manufacturing and assembly documentation, the Spreadsheet workbench pairs directly with the TechDraw workbench. Users can place cell ranges from a spreadsheet directly onto 2D technical drawing sheets as dynamic tables. This workflow allows for the inclusion of:

  • Revision tables: Tracking dates, changes, and author notes.
  • Cut lists: Detailing raw material lengths and stock requirements for fabrication.
  • Hole and tolerance tables: Listing explicit coordinate data and tolerances separate from the main drawing views to reduce drawing clutter.

When the spreadsheet updates, the linked table on the TechDraw sheet refreshes automatically, ensuring production-ready drawings remain accurate.

Recording Engineering Calculations and Design Intent

Beyond simple listings, the Spreadsheet workbench documents the mathematical logic and engineering decisions behind a design. Formulas, stress factors, safety margins, and unit conversions can be calculated and organized with descriptive labels. This creates an auditable record of design intent directly within the .FCStd project file, allowing collaborators and downstream engineers to verify assumptions and understand how critical dimensions were determined.

External Data Exchange and Reporting

Documentation often needs to extend beyond the CAD environment to procurement, ERP systems, and external stakeholders. The Spreadsheet workbench provides clean export capabilities to standard formats like .csv. This enables automated workflows where part lists, cost estimations, and specification sheets can be transferred directly to office suites, quoting software, or inventory databases without manual data re-entry.

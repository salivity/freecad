FreeCAD Built-In Simulation Capabilities

FreeCAD provides native simulation tools primarily centered around its integrated Finite Element Method (FEM) Workbench and assembly-level motion constraints. This article outlines the core simulation tools available directly within the FreeCAD application, detailing its structural, thermal, frequency, and kinematic analysis workflows, along with the internal pre- and post-processing tools used to run and visualize these engineering calculations.

The FEM Workbench

The primary environment for physics-based simulation in core FreeCAD is the FEM Workbench. It allows engineers to apply real-world physics to 3D geometry, define material properties, apply constraints, and analyze the resulting physical behavior. Core simulation types supported by the built-in FEM infrastructure include:

  • Linear Static Stress Analysis: Evaluates structural deformation, stress distributions, and safety factors under mechanical loads such as force, pressure, torque, and self-weight gravity.
  • Thermal Analysis: Simulates heat transfer through conduction and calculates temperature distributions, thermal gradients, and steady-state thermal expansion.
  • Frequency and Modal Analysis: Identifies the natural frequencies and vibration modes of mechanical components to prevent resonance issues.
  • Coupled Thermo-Mechanical Analysis: Evaluates structural stresses generated by thermal expansion or temperature differences.

Solver Integrations in Core

FreeCAD handles the pre-processing and post-processing internally while relying on established open-source computational solvers that communicate directly through the core software architecture:

  • CalculiX: The default and most tightly integrated solver in FreeCAD. It handles structural, thermal, and modal calculations seamlessly without requiring external script preparation.
  • Elmer: Integrated into core FreeCAD to handle multiphysics simulations, including electrostatics and heat equations.
  • Z88: A specialized solver built into the core interface primarily used for linear mechanical analysis.

Integrated Meshing and Geometry Preparation

Simulation requires discretizing continuous geometry into finite elements. The core FreeCAD application provides direct integration with native and bundled meshing engines:

  • Netgen and Gmsh: Integrated directly into the FEM interface to generate tetrahedral meshes from solid geometry, allowing control over element sizes, refinement zones, and boundary definitions.
  • Constraint Application: Boundary conditions—such as fixed supports, planar constraints, cyclic symmetries, and prescribed displacements—are applied directly to geometric faces, edges, or vertices within the native model tree.

Kinematic and Motion Simulation

In addition to structural analysis, FreeCAD features native kinematic simulation capabilities through its integrated Assembly Workbench:

  • Constraint-Based Kinematics: By defining mechanical joints (such as revolute, prismatic, cylindrical, and fixed joints), users can simulate the range of motion and degrees of freedom in multi-part assemblies.
  • Rigid-Body Motion: The solver calculates positions, movement limits, and mechanical interferences without deforming the components, ensuring moving mechanisms function correctly prior to manufacturing.

Built-In Post-Processing and Visualization

Once a simulation completes, FreeCAD visualizes the results directly in the 3D viewport using its built-in VTK (Visualization Toolkit) pipeline:

  • Contour Plots: Displays color maps for Von Mises stress, principal stresses, displacement, and temperature.
  • Deformation Scaling: Magnifies geometric displacement to visually evaluate structural deflection.
  • Data Probing: Allows users to extract exact numerical values at specific nodes, edges, or cut-planes across the model.

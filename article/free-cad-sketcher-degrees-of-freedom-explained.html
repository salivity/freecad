FreeCAD Sketcher Degrees of Freedom Explained

In FreeCAD's Sketcher workbench, the degree of freedom (DOF) counter indicates the number of ways geometric elements within a sketch can still move, resize, or rotate. It serves as a real-time diagnostic tool that guides designers toward achieving a fully constrained sketch, ensuring that 2D profiles behave predictably when modified or converted into 3D solid features.

The Role of Degrees of Freedom in Sketching

Every geometric entity drawn in a 2D sketch starts with fundamental degrees of freedom:

  • Points: Can move independently along the X and Y axes (2 degrees of freedom).
  • Lines: Have endpoints that can shift position, length, and angle (typically 4 degrees of freedom).
  • Circles: Can alter center coordinates and radius (3 degrees of freedom).

As you apply geometric constraints (such as horizontal, vertical, tangent, or coincident) and dimensional constraints (such as length, distance, or radius), you eliminate these freedoms.

Core Purposes of the DOF Counter

  1. Quantifying Design Constraint Status The DOF counter in the Tasks panel provides an exact metric of how close a sketch is to being mathematically locked down. A counter showing zero indicates the sketch is "Fully Constrained," which is the gold standard in parametric CAD design.

  2. Diagnosing Unconstrained Elements In complex sketches, finding the remaining moving parts can be difficult. Clicking the highlighted text next to the DOF counter prompts FreeCAD to highlight the unconstrained elements—usually in bright green—allowing you to quickly identify which vertices or lines lack definitions.

  3. Preventing Topological and Parametric Errors Unconstrained elements can shift unintentionally when parameters, formulas, or parent features change later in the modeling process. By using the DOF counter to verify that all freedoms are removed, you prevent downstream failures in operations like pads, pockets, and revolutions.

  4. Avoiding Over-Constraining The solver monitors the balance of definitions. If you add conflicting or redundant constraints, the counter alerts you to an "Over-constrained" state, indicating that constraints must be removed or toggled to reference dimensions to resolve mathematical conflicts.

Purpose of the FreeCAD Inspection Workbench

The FreeCAD Inspection workbench is a specialized toolset designed to evaluate, verify, and detect geometric deviations between different 3D models. This article outlines the core purpose of the workbench, detailing how it compares physical 3D scan data against nominal CAD models, its role in quality assurance and reverse engineering, and how its visual analysis tools function.

The primary purpose of the Inspection workbench is deviation analysis. In manufacturing and engineering, physical parts rarely match their ideal CAD representations with absolute perfection. By importing 3D scan data—such as point clouds or surface meshes obtained from optical 3D scanners or coordinate measuring machines (CMM)—users can overlay the real-world geometry onto the original nominal CAD design to verify manufacturing accuracy.

The workbench operates by calculating the Euclidean distance between points on a test object (usually a mesh or point cloud) and the surfaces of a reference object (usually a solid CAD shape). Rather than requiring manual dimension checks across hundreds of individual features, the tool automates distance calculations across the entire surface area of the component.

A central feature of the Inspection workbench is its color-mapped visual feedback. After running a comparison, the software displays a color spectrum directly on the geometry. Areas where the manufactured part has excess material are typically shown in warm colors (like red or orange), areas where material is missing or undercut appear in cool colors (like blue), and areas that fall within acceptable tolerance limits are displayed in green. This visual heatmap allows engineers to immediately identify warping, shrinking, tool wear, or machining errors.

The Inspection workbench serves three main workflows:

  • Quality Control and Assurance: Verifying whether a manufactured, cast, or 3D-printed component satisfies specified geometric tolerances before being deployed or delivered.
  • Reverse Engineering Validation: When recreating a CAD model from a digitized physical object, the workbench is used to ensure the newly created parametric solid faithfully represents the original scan data.
  • Deformation and Wear Analysis: Comparing scans of a mechanical component taken before and after operational stress testing to identify structural deflection, fatigue, or material erosion over time.

By providing native geometric comparison tools, the FreeCAD Inspection workbench bridges the gap between digital parametric design and real-world physical verification without requiring expensive proprietary metrology software.

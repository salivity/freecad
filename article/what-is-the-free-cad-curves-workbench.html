What Is the FreeCAD Curves Workbench?

This article provides an overview of the FreeCAD Curves workbench, an advanced community-developed add-on tailored for complex surface modeling and Non-Uniform Rational B-Splines (NURBS) curve manipulation. It explores the workbench's primary purpose, key features, and how it bridges the gap between traditional parametric solid modeling and complex freeform shape generation.

The Core Purpose of the Curves Workbench

FreeCAD’s built-in workbenches, such as PartDesign and Part, are optimized for prismatic, parametric solid modeling—operations based on standard extrusions, pockets, revolves, and basic chamfers. However, designing aerodynamic surfaces, ergonomic consumer products, or organic shapes requires sophisticated mathematical curve and surface control.

The Curves workbench was created to bring advanced NURBS curve generation and complex boundary surface design to FreeCAD. It gives designers fine control over continuous mathematical curves, enabling the creation of smooth, aesthetically pleasing, and aerodynamically efficient shapes that default solid modeling tools cannot achieve.

Key Features and Capabilities

The Curves workbench provides a specialized toolkit categorized into curve manipulation, surface creation, and surface analysis:

  • Gordon Surfaces: One of the most powerful features of the workbench is the Gordon surface tool. It allows users to generate a continuous, smooth 3D surface from an intersecting network (grid) of guide curves, maintaining curvature continuity across the entire surface patch.
  • Curve Creation and Modification: The workbench includes tools to interpolate points, draw parameterized B-splines, approximate complex geometries, split or join existing curves, and generate curves by intersecting faces.
  • Curve Projection: Users can project 2D or 3D curves directly onto complex curved faces, an essential operation for laying out trim lines, decals, or secondary features on curved hulls and bodies.
  • Surface Blending and Continuity: It provides functions to blend distinct surfaces while preserving continuity (G1 tangency and G2 curvature), preventing visible seams and structural stress concentrations.
  • Profile Sweeping and Skinning: Unlike basic lofting tools, the Curves workbench supports advanced sweeping methods, such as swept surfaces guided by multiple rails and spines with precise orientation controls.
  • Discretization and Analysis: The workbench includes curvature comb tools to visually inspect curve and surface quality, ensuring there are no unwanted inflections or flattening in critical regions.

Common Use Cases

The Curves workbench is widely used across engineering and design fields that demand organic or fluid geometry:

  • Automotive and Aerospace Design: Crafting body panels, fairings, wings, and aerofoil geometries that require strict aerodynamic profiles.
  • Marine Architecture: Lofting smooth ship and boat hulls from waterlines, buttocks, and station curves.
  • Ergonomic Product Design: Developing comfortable handgrips, mouse housings, and wearable technology.
  • Reverse Engineering: Converting raw point clouds or mesh cross-sections into editable, high-quality CAD surfaces.

Summary

The FreeCAD Curves workbench transforms FreeCAD from a purely mechanical solid modeler into a viable tool for industrial design and complex surface styling. By providing intuitive access to advanced NURBS math, it enables creators to build flowing, high-continuity surfaces that seamlessly integrate into wider parametric assemblies.
